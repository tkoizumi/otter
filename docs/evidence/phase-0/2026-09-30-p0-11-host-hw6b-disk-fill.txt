--- what this actually establishes ---
The failure is loud in the JOURNAL and silent everywhere an operator normally looks:

  the run list        says "succeeded"
  /health             says ok
  systemd             says active
  the captured output is incomplete, and only the journal says so

So the disk-check this task adds is not a convenience. It is the only surface that would
report this condition at all, and it reports it by SILENCE: the check stops pinging its
dead-man service, and that service own grace timer raises the alarm. A check that could
not measure -- no data directory, no curl, a full filesystem -- must go quiet for the same
reason, because a host that cannot write may also be unable to send.

It is also the sharpest argument for the free-space floor: the daemon reports success
while losing a job output, and 10 MiB of headroom looked like plenty right up until a
job produced more than that.

--- limits of this drill ---
The dead-man ALERT itself is not exercised end to end: OTTER_DISK_URL needs an external
service that does not exist, the same missing piece as HW-4b heartbeat URL. What the
artifact drill (scripts/drill/disk-pressure.sh) proves is the check going silent when a
threshold is crossed, against a stand-in listener that records the pings.
