The run was throttled at memory.high (27,821 high events) and peaked at 628 MiB --
BELOW the 711 MiB hard cap. So the hard cap never bound, and the OOM killer never ran.

--- and the host did not stay responsive ---
free -m:   Mem: 904 total, 868 used, 35 available
Swap:      2047 total, 2047 used, 0 free   <-- the 2 GiB swapfile, exhausted

api:       otter runs runaway --limit 5 -> context deadline exceeded

So the runaway survived, filled swap completely, left 35 MiB available, and starved
otterd until its API stopped answering. That is the opposite of the claim.

--- why the container drill passed and this does not ---
The committed drill sets MEMORY_HIGH=off and runs in a container with no swap, so the
hard cap is the binding constraint and the OOM kill fires in seconds. On the host both
conditions differ, and either alone is enough to change the outcome:

  1. MemoryHigh=60% throttles and reclaims first (the drill comment predicts exactly
     this: "can keep a hold-everything allocator pinned below the hard cap").
  2. No MemorySwapMax is set, so the cgroup swaps without bound. The 2 GiB swapfile,
     added by the stack as "insurance for a 1 GiB host", is what lets the runaway grow
     past RAM while memory.current stays under memory.max. Swap pressure then starves
     every other process in the same cgroup, including otterd, because otterd and the
     jobs share one cgroup by design (MemoryMax is documented as bounding the sum).

--- recovery (performed immediately) ---
The API was unresponsive, so the run could not be cancelled through it. Killed the
runaway child, restarted the unit to clear the cgroup, removed the job from the live
workspace. After recovery:
  Mem: 904 total, 268 used, 624 free, 635 available
  Swap: 2047 total, 0 used
  /health: {"status":"ok","version":"v0.3.0-rc1"}
  jobs: the two shopify jobs; the runaway job is gone
  schedules: both still paused (the pause survived a second restart)

--- a defect in the drill itself, found here ---
The drill asserts "no runaway process survived" with "pgrep -af runaway/main.py".
That pattern does not match: the child runs as plain "python3" with its cwd set, so
the assertion passes even while the runaway is alive. It reported clean on the first
check after the failure, and the runaway was in fact still running and holding 615 MiB.
The assertion is not load-bearing as written and must be fixed (match the cgroup procs
or the run id, not a path substring).

--- verdict ---
Proves: the caps are correctly configured and in force (the cgroup matches the unit),
and that the P0-04 guarantee does NOT hold as deployed on the Castor host. A runaway job
is throttled and swapped rather than killed, and it degrades the daemon and the host.

Does NOT prove: that the caps are useless. They bound the blast radius (memory.current
never exceeded memory.max, so the kernel never had to reclaim globally). What is missing
is a swap bound: with MemorySwapMax unset, the hard cap cannot be reached.

Remedy to verify next: emit MemorySwapMax (0, or a small fraction of MemoryMax) in
UnitFile so the cgroup cannot swap, making the hard cap binding again. That needs a code
change plus a re-run of this window, and it interacts with the stack's swapfile decision:
unbounded cgroup swap converts a contained OOM kill into a host-wide thrash.
